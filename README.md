# Zinn Hub Reviews: A Buyer's Guide to Sources, Ratings and Protection

**Zinn Hub** is a freelance services marketplace run by the UK company Zinn Digital Ltd (Companies House no. 16385785). **Zinn Hub Reviews** is its official review site, also run by Zinn Digital Ltd. This repository is a plain, open guide for anyone reading Zinn Hub reviews before placing an order: where each review comes from, how the averages are worked out, and what protection a buyer has if an order goes wrong.

## Where Zinn Hub reviews come from

**Marketplace order reviews.** On [Zinn Hub marketplace](https://zinnhub.com/), a buyer can only leave a review once an order is complete. That makes each review tied to a real purchase, which is strong evidence that the seller delivered something. It says less about orders that were cancelled or refunded, because those rarely end in a review.

**Trustpilot.** Anyone can post, whether or not they bought anything. The sample is small and includes critical reviews, several with replies from Zinn Hub. Trustpilot says it gives more weight to recent reviews when it calculates a TrustScore.

**Google.** Also open to anyone with an account. Seven reviews is a very small sample, so one new review can move the average noticeably.

**Testimonials.** Customers who agreed to be quoted. These are useful for detail, but they are chosen, so treat them as examples rather than evidence of typical results.

## Why the scores differ by source

A 4.9 marketplace average and a 3.4 TrustScore can both be accurate. They measure different groups of people:

1. **Who writes.** Order reviews come only from buyers who finished an order. Open platforms attract people with a strong reason to post, often after a problem.
2. **What is shown.** The marketplace list on the review site starts at four stars and says so. Trustpilot and Google show every rating.
3. **Sample size.** Tens of thousands of order reviews against a dozen open reviews. A small sample swings easily.

The sensible approach is to read both: order reviews tell you whether a seller delivers, and the critical open reviews tell you what to watch for if something goes wrong.

## Checking a seller before you order

This takes about fifteen minutes:

1. Read the reviews for your **service category**, not the whole marketplace. Zinn Hub has 12 categories, and quality varies between them.
2. Read the reviews on the **exact service** you plan to buy, and check the dates.
3. Open the **seller's store** and look at response times, revisions offered and delivery times.
4. Read the **critical reviews** on Trustpilot and Google, and Zinn Hub's replies.
5. Send the seller a short question before you pay. The reply tells you a lot.
6. Know your **buyer protection** before you order (next section).

A fuller version is the [Pre-order review checklist](https://zinnhub-reviews-hub.s3.amazonaws.com/zinn-hub-reviews-check-before-you-order.html), and the article [How to Check Zinn Hub Reviews Before You Hire a Freelancer](https://zinn-hub-reviews.blogspot.com/2026/10/how-to-check-zinn-hub-reviews-before.html) works through one example from start to finish.

## How buyer protection works

Based on Zinn Hub's [refund policy](https://zinnhub.com/refund-policy/), checked 1 Oct 2026:

| Step | What happens |
| :--- | :--- |
| Payment | Your payment is held by Zinn Hub, not paid to the seller, until you accept the work. |
| Delivery | You have five days to check the delivery. Weekends count. |
| Revisions | You can ask for changes within the revisions the service offers. |
| Cancellation | An order can be cancelled by agreement between buyer and seller. |
| Refund | Approved refunds go to your Zinn Hub wallet. |
| Dispute | If you cannot agree, Zinn Hub staff review the case. |

Your statutory rights still apply. The UK government's [consumer protection rights](https://www.gov.uk/consumer-protection-rights) page is a good starting point.
